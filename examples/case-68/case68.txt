What H-1B consular officers actually care about (from reviewing multiple interview experiences)
I've been helping people prepare for H-1B stamping interviews and noticed the same patterns keep coming up. Sharing what actually matters vs what people waste time worrying about.

What officers care about most:

Can you explain your job in plain English? If you say "I work on enterprise-level cloud-native microservices architecture" and the officer asks "so what do you actually do every day?" and you freeze — that's a problem. Practice saying your job duties like you're explaining it to a smart person who isn't in tech.

Do you know your employer? Sounds obvious but a shocking number of people can't answer basics like how many employees the company has, what the company does, or how long it's been in business. If you're going through a consulting firm, you NEED to know both your employer and the end client clearly.

Salary consistency. Whatever number you say has to match your petition exactly. Don't round up, don't include bonuses unless asked. Just state the base.

Degree-to-job connection. "How does your degree in mechanical engineering qualify you for this software role?" If you can't draw a clear line, that's a specialty occupation red flag.

What people worry about but usually doesn't matter:

- Wearing a suit vs business casual (doesn't matter, just look professional)

- Having too many documents (bring everything, they'll ask for what they need)

- The interview being long (most are 3-5 minutes)

- Green card intent (H-1B has dual intent — this is NOT F-1, you won't get denied for mentioning GC)

The #1 mistake I see:

People prepare answers in their head but never say them out loud. Then at the window they hear their own voice for the first time on these questions and it sounds completely different from what they rehearsed mentally. Practice out loud at least 5 times. Record yourself if you can.

Consulting/staffing firm applicants — extra prep needed:

- Know your end client name, project name, your specific role

- Have the client letter or SOW ready

- Be ready for "why can't an American do this job?"

- If your LCA wage level is Level 1, be ready to explain why
