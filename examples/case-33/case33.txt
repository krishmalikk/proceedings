Hi, this is my turn to give back to the community which I’ve learned so much from here.

I applied for a B1/B2 visa, and getting an appointment felt as difficult as going to the moon. A Reddit user shared a useful tip about checking for slots around 00:00 and 00:30 each hour. That strategy really helped me secure my initial appointment but that was on October. Since

I have some emergency in the U.S., I requested an emergency appointment, which got approved in three days after the request. I completed my biometrics in Chennai and attended my consulate interview in Delhi (today).

The biometric process was smooth and took less than 10 minutes. There was a locker facility available for ₹50. One important tip I want to share without going into too much detail: DO NOT KEEP YOUR CASH IN YOUR BAG WHILE I N THE LOCKER. You are allowed to carry it with you during biometrics, so keep it with you. I repeat, do NOT leave it in your bag.

Now, about my experience in Delhi

Honestly, I didn’t expect such a huge crowd. There were probably around 500 people to my knowledge, and I had an early slot 8:00Am. You’ll only be allowed in close to your scheduled time. Like, if your slot is 8:30, or 8:20. People are grouped in batches (like 8:00–8:30), and if your slot is after the mentioned time(there will be a time card which is hanged on the top of curtain where you will be standing), you’ll be asked to wait across the road until your batch is called. Don’t panic if you think you’ve missed your time,they manage it well. The security staff were calm, polite, and handled everyone smoothly.

Try to book early slots it helps you avoid the heat. I didn’t use the locker in Delhi since I stayed at a hotel nearby. I stayed at Diplomat Hotel, which is about a 15-minute walk or 5-minute cab ride from the embassy (recommended by a Reddit user in this sub). They provide breakfast, and it was good. I chose to eat at the hotel because I didn’t want to risk getting sick before or during the interview like stomach upset or dizziness. It’s better to eat properly rather than go on an empty stomach or rely on outside food.

Be prepared to stand in queues for most of the process. After the security check, you may get a chance to sit for about 15 minutes. Overall, the entire process from joining the queue to finishing the interview took around 2 hours.

For the interview, I was asked the usual questions: why I want to go to the U.S., which places I plan to visit, how long I will stay, and who is sponsoring the trip. My case was a bit different, so my interview lasted about 12 minutes. Interviewer was calm and kind person who took a time to hear my explanation and approved the visa.

For transport, there are autos available outside the embassy. Some charge around ₹50, but a few may ask for ₹150 negotiate if needed. I used Rapido bike and paid only ₹12, which was much cheaper.

That’s my experience, I hope this helps someone. In
